You're helping me write the story for a short interactive cartoon in a free safety-education app for kids. Most of the viewers are autistic, and some have other disabilities. They're mostly ages 4–8 (Pre-K to 3rd grade). Please write me a **better story outline and script** for the episode below.

## How the cartoon works (keep this format)
- It plays like a TV episode, then **pauses on a question**, and the child taps an answer.
- **Wrong answer:** the story acts out what happens next, gently and realistically, never scary. Then the lesson's superhero swoops in, rewinds time, and gives a one-sentence **Hero Tip**. The child tries again with that answer crossed out.
- **Right answer:** the safe version plays out and the story continues.
- There are **3 questions** per episode, plus a cold open, a happy ending and a short sign-off.
- Total length is about 4–6 minutes if the child gets everything right.

## The lesson for this episode: "Someone I Don't Know" (stranger safety, Level 1, ages 4–6)
The three questions must teach these three ideas, in this order:
1. Someone you don't know is close by. **Stay back a little and watch.** Don't go close, and don't run off and hide without telling anyone.
2. They offer you something, like candy. **Say "No, thank you!"** and don't take it.
3. They ask you to come with them, for example to see a puppy in their car. **Say "No!" and go straight to a grown-up you trust, and tell them** what happened and what the person looked like.

Each question needs 2–3 answer choices. Every wrong answer needs its own short "what happens next" consequence (2–3 beats) and its own Hero Tip.

## Characters (keep their looks, but you may improve their personalities and jokes)
- **Alex:** a little bunny, about 5, in a yellow hoodie. Curious and bouncy, makes understandable mistakes, says "Boing!", has a kid's voice. Alex looks at the viewer and asks "What should I do?" before each question.
- **Nutty:** an orange squirrel sidekick and the comic relief. He always notices trouble first, and his running gag is dropping acorns on his own head ("Oof! Nuts!").
- **Captain Caution:** an owl superhero in a green mask and cape. Calm and kind, never lectures. Saves the day, rewinds time, leaves Hero Tips. Signs off with "Be safe, be super!"
- **Dad:** Alex's dad, a tall bunny in a straw sun hat. Goofy, warm, plays along with games, tells bad jokes.
- **The stranger:** a goat in a cardigan and round glasses. **Must look and act like an ordinary, friendly person**, never scary or mean, because real strangers look like anyone. The unsafe thing is the situation, never the kind of animal.
- You can suggest one or two new characters if they make the story better.

## Setting
Right now it's "Sunny Meadow Park": a fairytale park with a castle-tower slide, swings, a duck pond, a picnic spot and a parking area with a car. You can suggest a different everyday place (a farmers market, a library, a fair, a beach) if it fits the story better. **Every part of the setting should make sense in the story.**

## Tone and rules
- A warm family cartoon. Think of the feel of Bluey or Arthur: real everyday moments, pretend play, humor that comes from the characters, and a heartfelt ending. **Do not copy any real show's characters, names or plots.**
- Short, simple sentences: most lines 12 words or fewer. Say "grown-up", not "trusted adult".
- Slow and clear. No sarcasm, no confusing figures of speech, no fast back-and-forth.
- Consequences stay gentle. The worst is something like "Dad can't find Alex" or "Alex feels mixed up". Nothing scary.
- Humor comes from the characters and never from the unsafe moment.
- Emotional arc: curiosity, then a little tension, then relief and pride.
- Include a cold open: 3–5 seconds of something funny before the title.
- End with a happy family moment and a sticker the viewer earns.
