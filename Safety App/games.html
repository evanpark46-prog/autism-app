Safety Games

Original games built right into Safety Superheroes, plus a few well-made picks from trusted organizations — playful ways to practice what you’ve learned.

Play with your heroes

Hero Movies

Interactive cartoons: watch the story, then YOU choose. See what happens next, and the hero rewinds time with a tip.

Hero Route

Watch your hero walk a safe route, then do it yourself — cross the street, get help when lost, escape smoke, and stay safe at the pool.

Spot the Danger

Tap the things that could hurt you in the kitchen, bathroom, outside and online. Don’t touch, move away, tell a grown-up!

Practice 911 Call

A pretend phone: dial 9-1-1 and practice what to say — what happened, where you are, your name, and stay on the line.

Freeze!

Your hero is walking — tap STOP the moment you see a stop signal, and GO only when it’s safe. Faster stops earn more stars!

Sign Match

A memory game with real safety signs: STOP, EXIT, poison, the walk signal and more. Flip two cards to find a match.

Who Can Help?

Lost at the store or the park? Tap the safe helpers — workers with name tags, police officers, a mom with kids.

Pack the Go-Bag

A storm is coming! Pack the family emergency bag — water, flashlight, headphones, your comfort toy… but not the bowling ball.

Hero Quiz Rush

Answer questions from every lesson and keep your streak going.

Word Power Quiz

Match words to their meanings, with a bonus flying mission every 5 in a row.

Say It Like a Hero

Learn to say safety words out loud — listen, clap the syllables, then say it into the microphone.

Sort It Out

Sort safety choices into Safe Choice or Needs a Rethink.

For grown-ups: the hero games (Hero Route, Freeze!, Spot the Danger, Who Can Help?, Sign Match, Practice 911 Call and Pack the Go-Bag) follow approaches studied for teaching safety skills to autistic learners — the hero models each step first (video modeling), then the learner rehearses with instant, gentle feedback (behavioral skills training), and wrong answers always lead to the safe choice (errorless learning). Practice the same steps together in real life afterward: skills carry over best when they are rehearsed where they are used.
